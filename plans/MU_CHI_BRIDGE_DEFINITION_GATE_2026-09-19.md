# MU-CHI BRIDGE DEFINITION GATE — 2026-09-19

## Gate objective

Determine by hand, before any finite scan, whether the project’s degree-one scalar
\[
ge_1=\mu(g)e_1,\qquad \mu(g)\in\mathbf F_3^\times
\]
has an intrinsic meaning for actual automorphisms of the \(q=3\) rank-4 Demushkin group, and whether that intrinsic character has a natural identification with the canonical orientation
\[
\chi:G\to\mathbf Z_3^\times.
\]

## 1. Intrinsic line

For
\[
G=\langle x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4\mid x_1^3[x_1,x_2][x_3,x_4]\rangle,
\]
abelianization gives
\[
G^{ab}\cong \mathbf Z_3^3\oplus\mathbf Z/3,
\]
with the torsion subgroup \(T=\operatorname{Tor}(G^{ab})\cong\mathbf Z/3\) generated by \(\bar x_1\).

The torsion subgroup is characteristic in every abelian group under automorphisms. The natural map
\[
G^{ab}\longrightarrow G/\Phi(G)
\]
sends \(T\) injectively to a one-dimensional \(\mathbf F_3\)-subspace
\[
L\subset G/\Phi(G).
\]
Under the frozen generator convention, \(L=\langle e_1\rangle\).

Therefore the \(e_1\)-line is not merely a coordinate choice: it is the Frattini image of the canonical torsion subgroup of \(G^{ab}\).

## 2. Intrinsic \(\mu\)-character

Every \(\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)\) induces an automorphism of \(G^{ab}\), hence preserves \(T\), and therefore preserves \(L\).

Thus there is a canonical scalar
\[
\mu_{\mathrm{int}}(\phi)\in\mathbf F_3^\times
\]
defined by the action of \(\phi\) on \(L\).

Multiplicativity is automatic:
\[
\mu_{\mathrm{int}}(\phi\psi)
=\mu_{\mathrm{int}}(\phi)\mu_{\mathrm{int}}(\psi).
\]

Hence
\[
\mu_{\mathrm{int}}:\operatorname{Aut}(G)\to\mathbf F_3^\times
\]
is a genuine character.

This resolves the intrinsicity question for the line/scalar itself, independently of arbitrary free-group lifts.

## 3. Relation to the frozen GSp multiplier

Let \(g\) be the degree-one matrix of an actual automorphism in the frozen convention and suppose
\[
g^T Jg=\nu(g)J,\qquad ge_1=a e_1.
\]

Canonical orientation invariance gives
\[
\chi\circ\phi=\chi.
\]
With
\[
\chi(x_1)=\chi(x_3)=\chi(x_4)=1,\qquad
\chi(x_2)=(1-3)^{-1},
\]
the \(x_2\)-coefficient of \(\phi(x_2)\) is \(1\), and the \(x_2\)-coefficient of \(\phi(x_1)\) is \(0\). Modulo \(3\), the second column therefore has \(e_2\)-coefficient \(1\).

Pairing \(ge_1=a e_1\) with \(ge_2\) gives
\[
\nu(g)\langle e_1,e_2\rangle
=\langle ge_1,ge_2\rangle
=a\langle e_1,e_2\rangle.
\]
Therefore
\[
\nu(g)=a=\mu_{\mathrm{int}}(\phi)
\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_3^\times.
\]

This proves the frozen GSp multiplier agrees with the intrinsic torsion-line character on the actual automorphism image, subject only to the repository’s already-frozen pairing/matrix convention.

## 4. Relation to \(\chi\)

The two characters remain categorically different:
\[
\chi:G\to\mathbf Z_3^\times,
\qquad
\mu_{\mathrm{int}}:\operatorname{Aut}(G)\to\mathbf F_3^\times.
\]

For \(q=3\),
\[
\chi(G)\subset1+3\mathbf Z_3,
\]
so
\[
\chi\bmod3=1.
\]
Consequently \(\mu_{\mathrm{int}}\) is not the mod-\(3\) reduction of \(\chi\).

More strongly, the available hand derivation establishes only that \(\chi\)-invariance constrains the degree-one action sufficiently to identify the GSp multiplier with the scalar on \(L\). It does not produce a natural map
\[
\mathbf Z_3^\times\to\mathbf F_3^\times
\]
that turns \(\chi\) into \(\mu_{\mathrm{int}}\) (the ordinary reduction map sends \(\chi\) to \(1\)).

A genuine recovery theorem would therefore require additional structure, for example a canonically defined \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\)-module functorially extracted from the dualizing module whose scalar character reduces to \(\mu_{\mathrm{int}}\). No such construction has been established here.

## 5. Gate decision

### PASS
- The \(e_1\)-line is intrinsic as the Frattini image of \(\operatorname{Tor}(G^{ab})\).
- Actual automorphisms preserve that line.
- The scalar action defines a genuine intrinsic character \(\mu_{\mathrm{int}}\).
- Under the frozen convention, \(\mu_{\mathrm{int}}\) equals the degree-one GSp multiplier on the actual automorphism image.

### FAIL
- Direct identification \(\mu=\chi\).
- Identification of \(\mu\) with \(\chi\bmod3\).

### OPEN
- Whether there is a deeper, nontrivial dualizing-module construction that recovers \(\mu_{\mathrm{int}}\) from \(\chi\) together with additional canonical data.

### Computational consequence

**No finite scan is authorized or needed at this gate.**

The next hand task, if continued, is to inspect the dualizing module itself and determine whether its \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\)-action has a canonical finite quotient carrying \(\mu_{\mathrm{int}}\). If no such quotient exists canonically, the project should record that \(\mu\) is an intrinsic automorphism-of-abelianization invariant but not an orientation recovery invariant.
