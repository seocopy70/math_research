## Purpose

The next experiment is q=3 versus q=9. Before computing any J-value, the q=9 source must be defined and derived independently.

The key warning is that q=9 is a genuine finite-q presentation:

\[
G_9=\langle x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4\mid x_1^9[x_1,x_2][x_3,x_4]=1\rangle.
\]

The degree-3 object must therefore not be assumed to equal the q=3 object, and it must not be assumed to be nonzero. In particular, because q=9 is a higher Zassenhaus degree than q=3, the x_1^9 power contribution may be invisible at degree 3. This is a hypothesis to be derived, not a preregistered numerical outcome.

- \(d_9=0\);

- \(d_9\ne0\);
- any particular coefficient pattern;
- any particular J-value.

The fact that 9=3^2 makes a degree-9 restricted-power contribution mathematically plausible, but the protocol does not turn that observation into the result.

## Next gate: locate the first nontrivial q=9 degree

The degree-3 probe is now closed as degenerate. The next question is not yet to compute a new invariant, but to determine the **first filtration degree at which the finite-q power term can contribute**.

For the fixed q=9 presentation, the Magnus power term is
\[
x_1^9=(1+X_1)^9
\]
over \(\mathbb F_3\). The elementary characteristic-3 calculation shows that its terms of degrees 1 through 8 vanish, with the first possible power contribution at degree 9. This explains why the degree-3 Gate-A difference vanished, but the exact degree-9 leading term of the *baseline-relative control* must still be derived from the full relation/control expression; it is not to be inserted as an assumed answer.

### Gate C — first nontrivial q=9 source

1. the degree-9 initial contribution of the q=9 control;

No assumption is made that \(\Delta_9(9)=X_1^{[9]}\), that the source is nonzero, or that the existing degree-3 map \(N\) is applicable. Those are separate questions to be derived and checked.

Only after Gate C is PASS may a new downstream invariant be designed. The existing degree-3 \(N/J\) probe is frozen and is not silently repurposed for degree 9.
