# HARD ATTACK 37 — PD²-INDEPENDENT SELECTOR UNIQUENESS
## 2026-09-20

### Purpose

This attack tests the strongest remaining logical question after Hard Attacks 33–36:

> Can uniqueness of the finite selector
> \[
> S_k(G)=\{\rho:G\to U_k:\ |\operatorname{coker}\delta_{k,\rho}|=3^k\}
> \]
> be proved from the finite carrier \((Q_k,M_k,E_k,\rho)\) itself, without using the external PD² orientation criterion?

The distinction is load-bearing. Hard Attacks 33–36 establish finite recognition and selector coherence under the PD² framework, but the canonical threshold \(3^k\) is currently justified externally by finite PD² top cohomology.

### 1. Pre-check

Object: rank-4 pro-3 Demuškin standard family
\[
G_q=\langle x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4\mid x_1^q[x_1,x_2][x_3,x_4]\rangle.
\]

Input carrier:
\[
N=P_{k+1}(G),\quad Q_k=G/N,\quad
M_k=N/(N^{3^k}[N,N]),\quad
E_k=G/(N^{3^k}[N,N]).
\]

Candidate coefficient action:
\[
\rho:Q_k\to U_k=1+3\mathbf Z/3^k.
\]

Functoriality/gauge requirement: the selector must be defined from the extension and transgression, not from a chosen presentation or known \(q\)/\chi formula.

Target: decide whether the maximal coker condition can be made internally explanatory.

### 2. First structural observation

The coker itself is intrinsic once the finite extension and coefficient action are fixed:
\[
\delta_{k,\rho}:\operatorname{Hom}_{Q_k}(M_k,A_k(\rho))
\to H^2(Q_k,A_k(\rho)),
\]
\[
C_k(\rho)=\operatorname{coker}\delta_{k,\rho}.
\]

Thus the question is not whether the coker is intrinsic. It is.

The question is whether the special value \(|C_k(\rho)|=3^k\) can be characterized without importing the fact that a Demuškin dualizing module has top cohomology of order \(3^k\) precisely at the canonical orientation.

### 3. Candidate internal route: compute the transgression image directly

For a one-relator model, a tempting internal mechanism is:

1. identify the finite transgression with the relator obstruction;
2. express its image as the subgroup generated by the coefficient-twisted relator row;
3. conclude that
\[
|C_k(\rho)|=3^k
\]
iff that row vanishes;
4. solve the row equations intrinsically to obtain the unique \(\rho_k\).

This would remove the PD² criterion from selector uniqueness.

However, this route has a serious gate: the identification in step 1–2 must be proved from the finite extension \((Q_k,M_k,E_k)\), not by importing the full one-relator Fox resolution and merely observing that it gives the same answer.

Otherwise it is presentation-dependent repackaging.

### 4. Exact obstruction row available for comparison

For the standard presentation, the twisted relator obstruction row previously computed is
\[
d_1=q+u_2^{-1}-1,\qquad d_2=0,
\]
\[
d_3=-\frac{u_4-1}{u_3u_4},\qquad
d_4=\frac{u_3-1}{u_3u_4}.
\]

Its vanishing gives
\[
u_2=(1-q)^{-1},\qquad u_3=u_4=1,
\]
with the relation forcing the corresponding finite candidate \(u_1\).

This establishes what an intrinsic finite-transgression calculation would have to reproduce, but **does not by itself prove that the finite carrier reproduces this row**.

### 5. Attack: is the PD² theorem actually needed for uniqueness?

There are now two logically distinct possibilities.

#### Route A — successful internal derivation

If the finite extension/transgression can be shown directly to have
\[
C_k(\rho)\cong A_k/\langle d_1,d_2,d_3,d_4\rangle
\]
(or an intrinsically equivalent quotient), then
\[
|C_k(\rho)|=3^k
\iff d_1=d_2=d_3=d_4=0
\iff \rho=\chi\pmod{3^k}.
\]

This would prove selector uniqueness without invoking the PD² top-cohomology cardinality criterion.

The remaining bridge from the selected finite character to the canonical PD² orientation would then be a separate identification theorem, rather than part of selector existence.

#### Route B — unavoidable PD² dependence

If every proof of the threshold/vanishing condition requires identifying the coker with \(H^2(G,A_k(\rho))\) and then invoking the Demuškin duality criterion, the PD² dependence is structural rather than merely an artifact of the current proof.

That would establish a useful logical boundary:
\[
\text{finite carrier} \to \text{obstruction selector}
\]
may be intrinsic, while
\[
\text{obstruction selector} \to \text{canonical orientation}
\]
requires PD² duality.

### 6. Critical attack on Route A

A hidden circularity must be avoided.

The standard one-relator resolution computes twisted \(H^2\), and the known PD² criterion then identifies the unique orientation. Reusing that resolution while calling its relator row a finite-transgression calculation would not be a genuinely new PD²-independent proof.

Therefore Route A is admissible only if the following finite-input reconstruction theorem is established:

> The class \(e_k\in H^2(Q_k,M_k)\), together with \(M_k\), determines the relevant twisted transgression image, and its evaluation can be computed from the finite extension itself, with no appeal to \(H^2(G,A)\), the dualizing module, the known \(\chi\), or a chosen relator coordinate.

This is precisely the missing theorem previously identified in Hard Attacks 28–30.

### 7. Result of the attack

No valid proof of that finite-input reconstruction was obtained in this pass.

In particular, the following implication remains unproved:
\[
(Q_k,M_k,E_k,\rho)
\Longrightarrow
\text{standard twisted relator row}.
\]

Conversely, no no-go theorem was obtained showing that such a reconstruction is impossible.

Therefore the correct classification is:

- coker object is intrinsic at fixed \(k\): **PASS / LOCAL**;
- PD²-independent selector uniqueness: **OPEN**;
- direct reuse of the standard Fox/one-relator row as a proof of intrinsic selector uniqueness: **FAIL / CLOSED as a proof shortcut**;
- finite-input reconstruction of the transgression obstruction: **OPEN / LOAD-BEARING**;
- PD²-based finite selector uniqueness: **PASS / LOCAL**;
- selector coherence under the PD² framework: **PASS / LOCAL**;
- carrier-only coherence: **OPEN**.

### 8. Important conceptual outcome

The attack sharpens, rather than weakens, the research boundary.

The remaining gap is no longer vaguely "find a better carrier." The precise missing theorem is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{finite extension data}
\;\Longrightarrow\;
\text{intrinsic evaluation of its 2-cell transgression}
}
\]

with enough information to determine the maximal coker condition.

If this theorem succeeds, the main result becomes substantially more intrinsic.

If it fails by a structural argument, that failure itself identifies PD² duality as a genuine logical boundary.

### 9. No-go/stop check

No evidence currently justifies closing the intrinsic selector route.

No evidence justifies claiming it is necessary to use PD².

Therefore this branch remains **OPEN**, and further work is authorized only by a concrete finite-input reconstruction mechanism or an admissible separation/no-go argument.

### 10. Relation to previous attacks

This attack supersedes neither Hard Attack 35 nor 36.

- Hard Attack 35: raw carrier tower need not exist.
- Hard Attack 36: selector tower is coherent under the PD² recognition theorem.
- Hard Attack 37: asks whether even finite-level selector uniqueness can be made PD²-independent.

The main theorem therefore remains:

\[
\boxed{
\text{finite coker recognition}
+
\text{PD}^2\text{ bridge}
+
\text{selector coherence}
\Longrightarrow
\chi_{\mathrm{filt}}.
}
\]

The stronger PD²-independent version is still OPEN.

### 11. Immediate next gate

Do not perform a numerical scan.

The next legitimate attack is to derive the transgression map directly from the finite extension class \(e_k\) and determine whether its image admits a basis-free finite presentation whose maximal-coker condition is computable without importing the infinite group's one-relator resolution.
