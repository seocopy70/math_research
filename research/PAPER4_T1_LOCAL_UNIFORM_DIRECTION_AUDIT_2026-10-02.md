# PAPER 4 — T1 LOCAL-UNIFORM DIRECTION TEST — 2026-10-02

## Decision

The first non-tautological T1 construction that directly targets the normalization functional has been identified.

The key observation is that the literature supplies a structural normalization mechanism at the **2-generator local level**: for a special edge (v,w), the subgroup generated by v,w is locally uniform, and its canonical orientation is determined by the group structure. In the oriented pro-l RAAG literature, a 2-generated oriented pro-l RAAG is either free or locally uniform; for a special edge the latter occurs, and the canonical orientation on the 2-generator subgroup is the restriction of the ambient canonical orientation. Proposition 4.11 also states that clique subgroups embed as oriented pro-l groups, with canonical orientation determined by the locally uniform subgroup structure. citeturn5search0

This suggests a finite-window analogue:

> detect, inside the adjacent window, those degree-one directions whose class admits a special-edge-type 2-generator local signature at the first nonzero defect depth; use the resulting distinguished directions in L_1/O_q to normalize the affine orientation hyperplane.

This is a genuine T1 attack because its output is directly the normalization datum, not a reconstruction of the whole directed graph.

## 1. Literature control

For a special edge (v,w), the defining relation is

  w v w^{-1}=v^{lambda(1)}

with v ordinary and w special, and the canonical orientation has

  theta(v)=1, theta(w)=lambda(1).

The same source records that the subgroup generated by the two vertices of an edge is locally uniform and that its canonical orientation is determined by the structure of the locally uniform subgroup. Proposition 4.11 extends this to clique subgroups. citeturn5search0

Theorem 4.9 states that, for an oriented pro-l RAAG, the Kummerian torsion-free orientation is precisely the canonical orientation when the graph is specially oriented. Thus the local orientation is not a presentation convention. citeturn0search0turn5search0

The literature does **not** prove the finite-window statement below. The transfer from the pro-p local-uniform structure to W_q <- W_{q+1} is the project-specific load-bearing step.

## 2. Finite target

Let

  O_q subset L_1

be the already established q-blind origin/torsion sector from RP-3, and put

  U_q=L_1/O_q.

The desired normalization is the unique functional

  omega_q in U_q^*

such that every special/sinkhole generator has value 1.

The new finite-window object should therefore not try to identify a full incidence graph. It should identify a distinguished set

  P_q subset U_q

of **normalized special directions**, with the intended property

  P_q={bar w : w is a special vertex}

up to the natural equivalences already present in the group.

If P_q spans U_q and is linearly compatible with the canonical values, then omega_q is the unique linear functional satisfying

  omega_q(P_q)=1.

This is target-first: P_q is only a recognition device for the affine normalization hyperplane.

## 3. Candidate definition: finite local-uniform signature

For each nonzero class u in U_q, define u to be T1-admissible if there exists a representative x in L_1 of u and a nonzero origin direction y in O_q such that the finite filtered subgroup generated by lifts of x and y has the following first-defect signature:

1. all lower commutator defects vanish after the intrinsic lower-degree sector is accounted for;
2. the first nonzero defect occurs exactly at q;
3. the q-defect is rank one and is normalized by the q-power of the origin direction y;
4. the resulting 2-generator finite quotient is the q+1 truncation of the special-edge semidirect model.

The formulation is intentionally existential in the representative x and origin direction y. This is essential: replacing x by x+o with o in O_q should not change its class in U_q, while a presentation-level choice of x is forbidden.

The proposed output is

  P_q={u in U_q : u has a finite special-edge local signature}.

No q is inserted into the definition. The relevant depth is discovered as the first nonzero defect degree, and the special-edge signature is tested relative to that intrinsic depth.

## 4. Why this is materially different from the failed J_q predicate

The failed criterion was

  J_q(u) != 0.

The complete one-sink counterexample showed that this is true for every nonzero u in L_1.

The present criterion asks for much more:

- a compatible origin direction;
- the special-edge two-generator extension type;
- rank-one first defect;
- normalization of that defect by the restricted-power class.

Thus a generic linear combination may be q-active without being T1-admissible.

In particular, the complete three-vertex example does not refute the present construction: for u=s+a the quotient class is the same as s modulo O_q, and the existential representative s restores the special-edge local signature. The failed arbitrary-linear purity problem is therefore not silently reintroduced.

## 5. Local model checks

### 5.1 Two-generator special edge

For

  G=<v,w | w v w^{-1}=v^{1+q}>,

O_q=<bar v> and U_q is one-dimensional, generated by bar w.

At W_q <- W_{q+1}, the relation gives the first nonzero defect

  [w,v]=v^q

and the q-power class P_q(bar v)=bar{v^q}.

The finite local signature therefore recognizes the unique nonzero class in U_q as normalized:

  omega_q(bar w)=1.

This reproduces the known local orientation coefficient directly from the finite extension mechanism, rather than merely quoting the canonical formula.

### 5.2 Complete one-sink model

For

  G=<s,a,b | [a,b]=1, sas^{-1}=a^{1+q}, sbs^{-1}=b^{1+q}>,

O_q=span(bar a,bar b) and U_q is one-dimensional.

Every representative s+o with o in O_q represents the same class bar s in U_q. Taking the pure representative s yields the special-edge local signature against either origin direction.

Hence the finite local test still gives

  P_q={bar s},

and therefore

  omega_q(bar s)=1.

This is exactly the normalization required by T1, while the previous J_q purity test fails on the same model.

### 5.3 Common-sink model

For

  G=<v_1,v_2,w | [v_1,v_2]=1,
      wv_iw^{-1}=v_i^{1+q}>,

O_q=span(bar v_1,bar v_2) and U_q is again one-dimensional.

The restricted q-power classes bar{v_i^q} give the normalization of the special-edge defect. The class bar w is therefore recognized and normalized by the same local signature.

### 5.4 Multiple special directions

For several special vertices, the candidate is deliberately not reduced to a linear rank condition. A sum of two special directions may produce a non-special 2-generator filtered pattern because the special vertices are not connected by ordinary edges. This is precisely the structural information lost by the bare J_q predicate.

A general proof that the resulting T1-admissible set spans the special quotient U_q, and contains no accidental directions, is still open.

## 7. Decisive success/failure theorem

The T1 candidate should now be tested against the following exact statement.

**T1-LU.** For every specially oriented pro-p RAAG in the declared class, at its first nonzero q-defect depth:

  P_q = {bar w : w is a special vertex}

inside U_q=L_1/O_q,

and P_q spans U_q.

Then the unique functional

  omega_q in U_q^*

with omega_q(bar w)=1 for every bar w in P_q

is intrinsic and determined by W_q <- W_{q+1}.

A failure can occur in two sharp ways:

1. an accidental direction u in U_q has the special-edge finite signature but cannot consistently be assigned the canonical value; or
2. two admissible groups have isomorphic adjacent windows but different induced normalized functionals.

Either one closes T1 at this window.

## 8. Important limitation

The local-uniform literature result is a **method transfer**, not a proof of T1. The cited literature works with the full pro-p group and its locally uniform subgroups; it does not say that the q+1 finite quotient remembers enough of the local-uniform canonical orientation.

Therefore the present result is not PASS.
