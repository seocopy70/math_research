# HARD ATTACK 59 — WHY THE MOD-9 CARRIER IS A COKERNEL — 2026-09-20

## 0. Strategic target

The current program asks whether the mod-9 projective carrier and the new P_4 residual should be understood as successive instances of one obstruction-theoretic construction.

The first attack is deliberately conceptual: explain why a coker appears, rather than merely observing one in a spectral-sequence calculation.

## 1. Critical distinction

A coker is not itself an orientation invariant.

For a cochain/deformation differential
\[
d:C^0\to C^1
\]
or a relevant transgression differential, the quotient
\[
\operatorname{coker}d=C^1/\operatorname{im}d
\]
is the universal target for maps out of C^1 that kill the gauge/lifting variations represented by \(\operatorname{im}d\).

Thus the meaningful statement is not
\[
\text{orientation}=\operatorname{coker}d,
\]
but rather:

> the intrinsic obstruction map annihilates the gauge image, hence factors uniquely through the cokernel.

This removes the main danger in the phrase “why coker?”: the coker is a universal quotient forced by the obstruction theory, while the orientation selector is an additional functional/vanishing condition on that quotient.

## 2. Obstruction-theoretic interpretation

At mod 9, the relation/power datum \((R,p)\) is detected by the twisted degree-(2,3) lifting obstruction
\[
\Theta_\lambda(f)=f(p)+(\lambda\wedge f)(R).
\]

The already proved naturality statement is exactly the type required for a quotient interpretation: presentation/lift changes are invisible to the obstruction family. Equivalently, the obstruction factors through the gauge quotient.

Therefore the structural chain is

\[
\boxed{
\text{raw relation/power deformation data}
\longrightarrow
\text{quotient by gauge/lift image}
\longrightarrow
\text{intrinsic obstruction carrier}
\overset{\Theta_\lambda}{\longrightarrow}
\text{orientation selector}.
}
\]

The coker is consequently an obstruction carrier, not the orientation itself.

## 3. Universal property

Let \(C\) be the raw finite deformation space and \(G\subseteq C\) the subspace generated by allowed gauge/lift changes. Then
\[
Q=C/G=\operatorname{coker}(d)
\]
has the following universal property:

For every vector space/module \(T\), every linear map
\[
F:C\to T
\]
with \(F(G)=0\) factors uniquely as
\[
C\twoheadrightarrow Q\xrightarrow{\bar F}T.
\]

The important research question is therefore not whether a coker can be written down—it always can—but whether the actual twisted obstruction is precisely a natural map satisfying \(F(G)=0\), and whether the resulting selector is non-tautological.

At mod 9 this is supported by the already closed degree-(2,3) twisted obstruction theorem and its presentation/gauge naturality.

## 4. What survives the hard attack

This gives a genuine conceptual explanation for the appearance of the coker:

\[
\boxed{
\text{coker} = \text{universal quotient that removes gauge directions before obstruction evaluation}.
}
\]

It also explains why “coker” alone was too weak a slogan. The orientation enters only through the family of obstruction functionals on the quotient.

The correct pattern is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{gauge complex}
\to
\text{obstruction carrier}
\to
\text{obstruction functional}
\to
\text{orientation zero/lifting condition}.
}
\]

## 5. Successive-stage conjectural form

This suggests a precise, testable obstruction tower rather than an analogy:

\[
C_2\xrightarrow{d_2}O_2\to Q_2
\]
for the first non-graded relation/power stage, followed by
\[
C_3\xrightarrow{d_3}O_3\to Q_3
\]
for the next P_4/power stage.

The desired relation is not that \(Q_2\) and \(Q_3\) are the same object. Rather, there should be compatible obstruction maps
\[
Q_2\to \text{mod-9 orientation data},
\qquad
Q_3\to \text{next lifting obstruction},
\]
with the second reducing to the first under the coefficient/filtration reduction map.

If this compatibility exists, the P_4 residual is a controlled enrichment of the same obstruction mechanism. If it does not, the failure identifies the precise point where the Layer-B mechanism stops.

## 6. Strong warning: do not overclaim “successive obstruction theory” yet

The above is a structural hypothesis, not yet a theorem for the full tower.

Three distinct things must still be proved:

1. the P_4 raw datum has a canonical gauge subspace whose quotient is intrinsic;
2. the next twisted lifting obstruction annihilates that gauge subspace;
3. reduction from the next stage to mod 9 is compatible with the already normalized obstruction functional.

Only (1)-(3) would justify calling the tower a single successive obstruction theory.

In particular, the existence of a coker in an LHS page or cohomology exact sequence is not enough by itself.

## 7. Consequence for the mod-27 normalization gate

The previous HA58 question was:

> Can the P_4 residual be normalized to the exact second logarithmic digit \(3e_2\)?

The coker perspective sharpens this.

Do not attempt to choose the scalar of the P_4 residual by comparing it directly with the known formula \(\chi(x_2)=(1-q)^{-1}\).

Instead, first seek a canonical coefficient-extension obstruction whose reduction is the established mod-9 obstruction. The required scalar, if canonical, must arise from compatibility of the connecting maps and the already fixed mod-9 normalization.

Thus the decisive test becomes:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Does the P_4 obstruction descend/reduce to the HA45 mod-9 obstruction under the coefficient extension map?}
}
\]

If yes, the scalar is potentially forced by functoriality rather than inserted from \(q\).

If no, then the projective P_4 direction is real but the scalar bridge requires additional structure.

## 8. Hard negative boundary

There is also a useful no-go criterion.

If two different coefficient-extension/gauge choices produce the same intrinsic P_4 projective class but different putative scalar digits, while both reduce to the same mod-9 carrier, then no scalar normalization can be extracted from the P_4 filtered datum plus Layer-B data alone.

That would prove a genuine logical boundary:
\[
\boxed{
\text{projective higher residual}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{normalized 3-adic digit}.
}
\]

This is stronger than merely saying “normalization is difficult.”
