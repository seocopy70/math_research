# Paper 5 Gate B — exact-identity candidate audit — 2026-10-07

## Trigger

After the nontrivial p=3, a=2 seed W=z^2 passed the full two-sided Magnus ideal-membership test through D9 (Actions run 37568611185), an exact group identity was proposed:
\[
z^{ap}=x^{ap}[x^a,y]
\]
in
\[
G=F/\overline{\langle r-1\rangle},\qquad r=z^p x^{-p}[x,y]^{-1}.
\]

The proposed route was to derive this from
\[
[x^p,[x,y]]=1
\]
and then conclude that \([x^a,y]=[x,y]^a\).

## Audit result

The first displayed centralizer identity is valid (with the chosen commutator convention), but the next inference is not.

Set \(c=[x,y]\). From the relator,
\[
c=z^p x^{-p}.
\]
Hence
\[
x^{-p}cx^p=x^{-p}z^p=c,
\]
so
\[
[c,x^p]=1.
\]

However, for \(a=2\),
\[
[x^2,y]=c^x c
\]
(up to the fixed convention/order), so
\[
[x^2,y]=c^2
\]
requires
\[
c^x=c,
\]
not merely
\[
c^{x^p}=c.
\]

For \(p=3\), \(c^{x^3}=c\) only says that conjugation by \(x\) acts on \(c\) with order dividing 3. It does not force the action itself to be trivial.

The subsequent Hall–Petresco/Magnus observations do not repair this gap. Statements such as
\[
[x^p,c]=1
\]
or
\[
[[x,y],x]\in G^p
\]
are filtered/power-level statements; they do not imply the exact group identity \([[x,y],x]=1\). Likewise, vanishing of the relevant class in an associated graded quotient cannot be promoted to an exact equality in \(G\) without an additional separation argument.

## Relation to D4-D9 computation

This audit does **not** contradict the D4-D9 PASS/LOCAL result for \(a=2,W=z^2\). That computation establishes only

The observed D4-D9 vanishing is therefore evidence that the defect is pushed into higher filtered layers. It is not evidence, by itself, that the defect is zero in the full normal closure.
