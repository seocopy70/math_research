## 1. Free-group degree-two model

Let F be the free pro-3 group on x_1,...,x_4, with the same lower 3-central series. In degree 2 the free restricted Lie object has

L_2(F)=P_2(F)/P_3(F)
      = V^{[3]} \oplus \Lambda^2 V,

so dim L_2(F)=4+6=10.

Choose the basis
t_i = x_i^3 mod P_3(F),  i=1,...,4,
c_{ij}=[x_i,x_j] mod P_3(F),  i<j.

The relator r lies in P_2(F), and its initial degree-two form is

rho_2 = t_1 + c_{12} + c_{34}.

This is nonzero, since its t_1 coefficient is 1.

## 2. Passage from F to G

The quotient G=F/<<r>> induces on the degree-two lower 3-central quotient the relation generated by the initial form rho_2. At this first nontrivial degree there are no additional independent initial relations: every conjugate contributes the same degree-two class, and multiplication by elements of F changes it only in degree >=3.

Hence

W=P_2(G)/P_3(G)
  ~= L_2(F)/<rho_2>,

and therefore

dim_F3 W = 10-1 = 9.

This also identifies the class-2 quotient Q_2=G/P_3 as a central extension

0 -> W -> Q_2 -> V -> 0

with W generated by the surviving four cube directions and six commutator directions modulo rho_2.

This is now a theorem at the frozen q=3 level, not merely a presentation-level dimension guess.

## 3. LHS transgression d_2

For the central extension above, the LHS transgression

d_2 : H^1(W,F_3)=W^* -> H^2(V,F_3)

is obtained by evaluating the central extension class on a functional phi in W^*.

For elementary abelian V and p=3,

H^2(V,F_3) ~= beta(V^*) \oplus \Lambda^2(V^*),

so dim H^2(V,F_3)=4+6=10.

Let q:F_2:=V^{[3]} \oplus \Lambda^2 V -> W be the quotient map. A functional phi in W^* is equivalently a functional \tilde phi on L_2(F) satisfying \tilde phi(rho_2)=0.

Under the standard central-extension transgression identification,

d_2(phi)
 = (\tilde phi(t_1),...,\tilde phi(t_4); \tilde phi(c_{12}),...,\tilde phi(c_{34}))

viewed in the corresponding beta-plus-cup basis of H^2(V,F_3), up to one common convention sign.

Thus d_2 is exactly the inclusion of the hyperplane

H = {\tilde phi in L_2(F)^* : \tilde phi(rho_2)=0}

into the 10-dimensional H^2(V,F_3) coordinate space.

Therefore

rank d_2 = dim H = 9.

Equivalently, the nine surviving central generators have linearly independent extension components.

## 4. The surviving line

Since rank d_2=9 and dim H^2(V,F_3)=10,

dim coker(d_2)=1.

Moreover the image is the hyperplane annihilating rho_2 under the natural evaluation pairing. Hence the quotient line is canonically represented by the relation-jet class rho_2 itself, after identifying

L_2(F) ~= H^2(V,F_3)^*

through the power/cup duality.

Thus the unique potential surviving H^2 line is exactly the degree-(2,3) relation jet

[(R,p)],

with

R=[X_1,X_2]+[X_3,X_4],
p=X_1^(1),

up to the already controlled common H^2 scaling/sign convention.

This is stronger than the earlier statement that (R,p) was merely a plausible survivor: the one-dimensional survivor is forced by the single defining relation.
