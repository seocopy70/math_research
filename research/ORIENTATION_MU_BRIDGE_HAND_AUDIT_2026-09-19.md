# Orientation bridge audit: mu versus chi — 2026-09-19

## Status
**HAND-DERIVATION — PRELIMINARY PASS for type/separation statements; no computational scan.**

## 1. Objects and domains

The canonical Demushkin orientation is an internal group character
\[
\chi:G\to\mathbf Z_3^\times.
\]
For the q=3 rank-4 presentation
\[
G=\langle x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4\mid x_1^3[x_1,x_2][x_3,x_4]\rangle,
\]
standard orientation theory gives
\[
\chi(x_1)=\chi(x_3)=\chi(x_4)=1,
\qquad
\chi(x_2)=(1-3)^{-1}=-\frac12.
\]
The image lies in \(1+3\mathbf Z_3\), so \(\chi\bmod3\) is trivial.

The project’s \(\mu\) is instead attached to a degree-one automorphism matrix
\[
g\in\operatorname{GSp}_4(\mathbf F_3),
\qquad ge_1=\mu(g)e_1,
\]
with \(\mu(g)\in\mathbf F_3^\times\). Thus the two objects have different domains and different coefficient groups.

## 2. The distinguished e1-line is potentially intrinsic

For the q=3 presentation,
\[
G^{ab}\cong \mathbf Z_3^3\oplus\mathbf Z/3,
\]
with the torsion subgroup generated by the class of \(x_1\). This torsion subgroup is characteristic in \(G^{ab}\). Its image in the Frattini quotient \(G/\Phi(G)\) is therefore a canonical one-dimensional \(\mathbf F_3\)-subspace.

Hence the line represented by \(e_1\) need not be dismissed as merely a coordinate artifact: under the frozen presentation-to-Frattini identification it is the image of the canonical torsion line. What remains to be checked is the exact compatibility of the project’s degree-one matrix convention with this intrinsic identification.

## 3. If g comes from an actual automorphism, chi-invariance constrains the e2 coefficient

Canonical orientation is functorial under group isomorphisms, so for every \(\phi\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)\),
\[
\chi\circ\phi=\chi.
\]
Write the degree-one action in the generator convention. If
\[
\phi(x_2)_{ab}=x_1^{b_1}x_2^d x_3^{b_3}x_4^{b_4},
\]
then orientation invariance gives
\[
(1-3)^{-d}=(1-3)^{-1}.
\]
Because \((1-3)^{-1}\) has infinite pro-3 order, this forces
\[
d=1
\]
in \(\mathbf Z_3\), hence also \(d\equiv1\pmod3\).

Similarly, \(\chi(\phi(x_1))=1\) forces the x2-coordinate of \(\phi(x_1)\) to vanish. Therefore an actual automorphism preserving the canonical torsion line has first column \(a e_1\), and the e2 diagonal coefficient is 1 modulo 3.

## 4. Consequence for the GSp multiplier under the frozen convention

Let the actual degree-one matrix satisfy
\[
g^T Jg=\nu(g)J,
\qquad ge_1=a e_1.
\]
The \((e_1,e_2)\)-pairing gives
\[
\langle ge_1,ge_2\rangle=\nu(g)\langle e_1,e_2\rangle.
\]
Since the e2 coefficient of \(ge_2\) is 1 modulo 3, this yields
\[
\nu(g)=a
\quad\text{in }\mathbf F_3^\times.
\]
Thus, **provided the repository’s generator-side GSp convention is exactly the one used in this calculation**, the project’s condition
\[
ge_1=\mu(g)e_1
\]
can be interpreted intrinsically for actual automorphisms as the scalar action on the canonical torsion line, and \(\mu(g)\) agrees with the GSp multiplier on the actual automorphism image.

This is a useful bridge, but it is a statement about an \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\)-character, not yet about \(\chi\).

## 5. What this does NOT prove

It does **not** prove
\[
\mu=\chi,
\]
or any direct recovery of \(\chi\) from \(\mu\). The type mismatch is fundamental:
\[
\chi:G\to\mathbf Z_3^\times,
\qquad
\mu:\operatorname{Aut}(G)_{\mathrm{line}}	o\mathbf F_3^\times.
\]
Moreover, for q=3,
\[
\chi(G)\subset1+3\mathbf Z_3,
\]
so \(\chi\bmod3=1\), whereas \(\mu\) may take the nontrivial value 2 in \(\mathbf F_3^\times\) on the broader GSp admissible category. Therefore \(\mu\) cannot simply be the mod-3 reduction of \(\chi\).

The only defensible relationship established so far is indirect: \(\chi\)-invariance constrains actual automorphisms strongly enough that, under the frozen matrix convention, their GSp multiplier is the scalar on the canonical torsion line.
