# Phase 2-5 — Direct WeylModules verification of Δ(2,1)

Date: 2026-09-15

## Purpose

Test the hypothesis that the 45-dimensional Sp_4(F_3)-module W obtained from the degree-4 Demuškin obstruction is related to the characteristic-3 tilting module T(2,1).

This phase independently constructs the Weyl module Δ(2,1) in characteristic 3 using Doty's WeylModules GAP package and compares its internal structure with the previously computed 10–25–10 structure of W.

## Direct computation

Doty's WeylModules package was cloned from the GitHub repository `srdoty/WeylModulesNew`, loaded into GAP, and used to construct:

```text
V := WeylModule(3,[2,1],"C",2);
```

The direct output was:

```text
Dim(V) = 35
MaximalVectors(V) = [ 1*v0,  y1*y2*v0 + y3*v0 ]
```

Thus Δ(2,1) has an additional maximal vector in characteristic 3 and is not simple.

The submodule generated by the second maximal vector was computed:

```text
S := SubWeylModule(V, m[2]);
Dim(S) = 10
Q := QuotientWeylModule(S);
Dim(Q) = 25
MaximalVectors(Q) = [ 1*v0 ]
```

Therefore the direct computation establishes the exact short exact sequence

```text
0 → L(2,0) → Δ(2,1) → L(2,1) → 0
```

with dimensions

```text
10 → 35 → 25.
```

As a control, Δ(2,0) was constructed directly:

```text
V20 := WeylModule(3,[2,0],"C",2);
Dim(V20) = 10
MaximalVectors(V20) = [ 1*v0 ]
```

so Δ(2,0) itself is simple in characteristic 3 and realizes L(2,0) of dimension 10.

## Consequences

The non-split Weyl-module extension gives

```text
Ext^1(L(2,1), L(2,0)) ≠ 0.
```

This is stronger than the earlier Cartan-matrix evidence. The Cartan entry linking the 10- and 25-dimensional simples was only evidence of modular interaction and did not determine Ext^1. The explicit non-split extension proves nonvanishing, but does NOT prove

```text
dim Ext^1(L(2,1),L(2,0)) = 1.
```

That dimension remains to be computed if needed.

## Comparison with the Demuškin-derived module W

Previously established by exact finite-field computation:

```text
dim W = 45
End_H(W) ≅ F_3[ε]/(ε^2)
N^2 = 0
rank(N) = 10
im(N) = U, dim(U)=10
ker(N) = K, dim(K)=35
M = K/U, dim(M)=25
U is irreducible
M is absolutely irreducible
```

Hence W has the exact internal filtration

```text
0 ⊂ U ⊂ K ⊂ W
```

with dimensions

```text
10 — 25 — 10
```

and the outer 10-dimensional layers are isomorphic via N:

```text
W/K ≅ U.
```

The WeylModules computation now independently produces exactly the same 10-25 pattern in Δ(2,1):

```text
L(2,0) → Δ(2,1) → L(2,1)
10       35          25
```

This makes the identification

```text
W ≅ T(2,1)
```

a strong hypothesis rather than a mere dimension coincidence, but it is NOT yet a proved isomorphism.

## Tilting-module status

Standard highest-weight/tilting theory gives the existence and uniqueness (up to isomorphism) of the indecomposable tilting module T(2,1) for the dominant weight (2,1).

The expected structure is

```text
L(2,0)
   |
L(2,1)
   |
L(2,0)
```

with total dimension 45, equivalently a Weyl filtration containing Δ(2,1) and Δ(2,0) once each.

However, the direct Weyl-module calculation alone does not prove that W is T(2,1), nor does it by itself establish the full 10-25-10 Loewy structure of T(2,1). The exact tilting character/Weyl filtration should be checked before treating that structure as computationally verified.

## Next decisive checks

1. Determine the Weyl filtration/character of T(2,1) and verify
   `(T(2,1):Δ(2,1))=1` and `(T(2,1):Δ(2,0))=1`, with no additional Weyl factors.
2. Verify that the 10-dimensional module U from W is isomorphic to L(2,0), not merely equal in dimension.
3. Verify that the 25-dimensional quotient M=ker(N)/im(N) is isomorphic to L(2,1), again by actual generator action/module invariants.
4. Compare W with T(2,1) directly, including the nilpotent endomorphism N and its rank-10 image.
5. Compute `Ext^1(L(2,1),L(2,0))` only if the exact extension multiplicity is needed; current result is only nonzero Ext^1.
6. Keep the distinction between Sp_4(F_3) and PSp_4(F_3) explicit in any finite-group identification.

## Research interpretation

The previously mysterious 10–25–10 structure is now strongly linked to a known characteristic-3 C2 highest-weight pattern. The safe current chain is:

```text
Demuškin relation
→ degree-4 target T
→ Sp_4(F_3)-orbit/module W
→ exact 10–25–10 indecomposable structure
→ characteristic-3 Δ(2,1) has 10-submodule and 25-dimensional simple quotient
→ strong hypothesis W ≅ T(2,1)
→ orientation question remains open
```

The identification with T(2,1), once proved, should be regarded as an intermediate representation-theoretic theorem. It does NOT by itself prove that the Demuškin orientation can be canonically recovered from the filtration.

## Important logical safeguards

- T itself is not canonical merely because W has a canonical-looking representation-theoretic structure.
- W^H=0 does not rule out canonical information in a quotient, submodule, extension class, invariant form, or endomorphism.
- Cartan multiplicity does not determine Ext^1 dimension.
- p-restricted highest weight alone should not be used as an unsupported assertion about restriction from the algebraic group to the finite group Sp_4(F_3).
- The statement `W ≅ T(2,1)` remains a hypothesis until module actions/filtrations are explicitly matched.
- The original orientation-recovery problem remains unresolved.
