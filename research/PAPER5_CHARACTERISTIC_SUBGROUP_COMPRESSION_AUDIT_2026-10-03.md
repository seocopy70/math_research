# PAPER 5 — CHARACTERISTIC SUBGROUP COMPRESSION AUDIT
## 2026-10-03

### Question
After the characteristic-kernel no-go, can the family of all admissible quotient kernels produce a smaller explicitly characteristic finite quotient via intersection or generated-subgroup constructions?

### Pre-check
**Object.** Let \(\mathcal K(W_n)\) be the set of kernels of all admissible realizations \(\pi:W_n\twoheadrightarrow H\), \(H\in\mathcal C_{d,n}\), with the intrinsic cup-radical \(H^1\)-kernel condition.

**Input.** Only the unmarked critical window \(W_n\) and the intrinsically defined admissible realization class.

**Functoriality.** \(\operatorname{Aut}(W_n)\) permutes \(\mathcal K(W_n)\), so both
\[
K_{\cap}:=\bigcap_{K\in\mathcal K(W_n)}K,
\qquad
K_{\vee}:=\langle K:K\in\mathcal K(W_n)\rangle
\]
are characteristic.

**Gauge.** These constructions deliberately quotient the entire admissible kernel orbit; no representative is chosen.

**Orientation bridge.** The only required bridge is preservation of the already orbit-invariant relative Boolean.

**q-blindness.** The construction uses the intrinsic admissible class, not an externally named \(q\).

**Separation.** The moving-kernel family from C2 is enough: \(K_0=\ker\pi_0\) and \(K_c=\ker\pi_c\), with \(\pi_c(z)=c\in D_2(Q_n)\), are distinct admissible kernels whenever \(c\neq1\).

**Stop.** No new carrier or threshold computation is authorized; this is a structural subgroup calculation.

## 1. The intersection characteristic quotient is too large

Because \(\operatorname{Aut}(W_n)\) permutes the admissible kernels,
\[
K_{\cap}=\bigcap K
\]
is characteristic. Hence \(W_n/K_{\cap}\) is a canonical characteristic quotient.

But \(z\in K_0\), while for any nontrivial \(c\in D_2(Q_n)\),
\[
\pi_c(z)=c\neq1,
\]
so \(z\notin K_c\). Therefore
\[
z\notin K_{\cap}.
\]
On the other hand \(z\in K_0\), and \(K_0\) is the kernel of the canonical admissible quotient \(W_n\twoheadrightarrow Q_n\). Thus
\[
K_{\cap}\subsetneq K_0
\]
and consequently
\[
|W_n/K_{\cap}|>|W_n/K_0|=|Q_n|
\]
for the finite critical window.

So the intersection construction does not recover \(Q_n\), nor does it compress to a smaller object. It retains precisely the kernel-motion information that C2 showed cannot be represented by a single canonical kernel.

## 2. The generated characteristic kernel collapses to the abelian shadow

Let
\[
K_{\vee}=\langle K:K\in\mathcal K(W_n)\rangle.
\]
This is characteristic and contains \(K_0\). Hence the quotient \(W_n/K_{\vee}\) is a quotient of
\[
W_n/K_0\cong Q_n.
\]

For every admissible \(c\in D_2(Q_n)\), the element \(zc^{-1}\) (where \(c\) denotes any lift in the quotient-coordinate notation; equivalently use the kernel element whose \(\pi_c\)-image is trivial) lies in \(K_c\). Under the canonical quotient \(\pi_0\), its image is \(c^{-1}\). Therefore the image of \(K_{\vee}\) in \(Q_n\) contains every \(c\in D_2(Q_n)\). Since \(K_0\subset K_{\vee}\), the image is exactly the normal subgroup generated by \(D_2(Q_n)\), namely \(D_2(Q_n)\) itself.

Thus
\[
W_n/K_{\vee}\cong Q_n/D_2(Q_n)=Q_n^{\mathrm{ab}}.
\]
In the declared Demuškin critical class this is the intrinsic degree-one/abelian shadow and carries no nonabelian extension-class information.

The construction therefore goes in the wrong direction for the research target: the intersection gives a canonical quotient strictly larger than \(Q_n\), while the generated-kernel construction gives a canonical quotient no larger than the abelianization. Neither yields the desired finite realization of the relative obstruction.

## 3. Stronger conclusion

The two universal characteristic operations on the entire admissible kernel orbit are now exhausted at the first natural level:
\[
\boxed{
W_n/K_{\cap}\;\text{is strictly larger than }Q_n,
\qquad
W_n/K_{\vee}\cong Q_n^{ab}.
}
\]

Hence there is no characteristic quotient obtained by taking the intersection or generated subgroup of all admissible kernels that is isomorphic to the target class \(\mathcal C_{d,n}\).

More importantly, these operations exhibit the structural obstruction to coarsening the realization groupoid by a characteristic quotient: preserving every realization forces one to retain extra kernel-orbit data, while identifying all realizations forces collapse to degree-one data.

## 4. Logical boundary

This does **not** prove that every conceivable characteristic finite invariant is either larger than \(Q_n\) or abelian. A more elaborate characteristic construction could, in principle, encode the realization groupoid without being a quotient formed directly from \(\mathcal K(W_n)\).

Therefore the correct classification is a bounded structural no-go, not an absolute coarsest-realization theorem.

The next legitimate question is whether the realization groupoid itself is already the natural categorical endpoint, or whether a characteristic object built from the *action of* \(\operatorname{Aut}(W_n)\) on \(\mathcal K(W_n)\) can encode the same one-component information more economically. Any such construction must be compared against the groupoid and must not select a characteristic kernel.
