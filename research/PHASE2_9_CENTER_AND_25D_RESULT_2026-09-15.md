# Phase 2-9 — Center action and identification of the 25-dimensional module

Date: 2026-09-15

## 1. Purpose

Phase 2-8B proved directly that the 25-dimensional quotient

`M = ker(N) / im(N)`

is irreducible for the five-generator action of `Sp4(F3)`:

`<A_1,...,A_5>_alg = Mat_25(F_3)`.

The next question is the precise identity of this irreducible module. A naive
identification with the algebraic-group highest-weight module `L(2,1)` was
considered because `Delta(2,1)` in characteristic 3 has a 25-dimensional
simple quotient. That identification is NOT established by the dimension
alone.

## 2. Exact center check

The center of `Sp4(F3)` is `{+I,-I}`. On the natural degree-1 space, `-I`
acts by the scalar `-1 = 2 mod 3`.

Every homogeneous Lie monomial of degree 4 therefore acquires the scalar

`(-1)^4 = +1 mod 3`.

Hence `-I` acts trivially on the entire degree-4 free Lie space `L_4`, and
therefore on its quotient `Q_4`, on the orbit module `W`, and on every
subquotient including `M`.

This is an exact structural calculation, independently of numerical rank
computations.

Therefore:

`M` is naturally a module for

`PSp4(3) = Sp4(3)/{+-I}`.

## 3. Consequence for the highest-weight label

The center check shows that one must not identify `M` with an
`Sp4(F3)`-module whose central involution acts nontrivially. In particular,
the label `L(2,1)` cannot be attached to `M` merely because both have
 dimension 25 in a characteristic-3 highest-weight calculation. The
conventions relating algebraic-group highest weights to the finite group
`Sp4(3)` must be checked explicitly.

This corrects the earlier overly-quick route toward `M ~= L(2,1)`.

## 4. Literature verification

Cabanes–Marin, *On Ternary Quotients of Cubic Hecke Algebras*, Proposition
4.10, explicitly identifies a unique simple module of dimension 25 for
`Sp4(3)/Z(Sp4(3))` in the relevant characteristic-3 setting. The same paper
also proves that the 25-dimensional irreducible representation of
`Sp4(F3)` is defined over `F3` and describes it as the 25-dimensional simple
module occurring in the relevant construction.

Thus our exact facts

- `dim M = 25`,
- `M` irreducible,
- `Z(Sp4(3))` acts trivially,

put `M` in precisely the finite-group isomorphism class singled out by that
literature result, subject to the exact hypotheses/conventions of Proposition
4.10.

The safe current identification is therefore:

`M ~= the 25-dimensional simple PSp4(3)-module of Cabanes–Marin.`

We do NOT yet translate this finite-group label into an algebraic-group
highest weight such as `L(2,1)`.

## 5. Relation to Phase 2-5

Phase 2-5 independently verified

`0 -> L(2,0) -> Delta(2,1) -> L(2,1) -> 0`

with dimensions `10 -> 35 -> 25` using WeylModules. That is a valid
statement about the characteristic-3 Weyl module `Delta(2,1)`.

It does NOT by itself identify the Demushkin-derived module `M` with the
25-dimensional quotient of `Delta(2,1)`.

The two constructions should remain logically separate until an explicit
finite-group/algebraic-group comparison is supplied.

## 7. Next decisive task

The next task is NOT to force a highest-weight label. It is to compare the
known 25-dimensional PSp4(3) module with the actual generator matrices of M,
preferably by an explicit intertwiner. If successful, this will give a fully
finite-group-level identification and only then allow a controlled translation
to highest-weight language.

A separate unresolved issue remains in the filtration/Hall–Petrescu bridge:
the A1 step requires an actual proof of `gr_3((uv)^3)=0` under the chosen
filtration. That issue must not be bypassed by the representation-theoretic
identification.
