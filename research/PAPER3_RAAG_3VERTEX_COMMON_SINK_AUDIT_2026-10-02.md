# PAPER 3 FOLLOW-UP — 3-VERTEX COMMON-SINK TEST — 2026-10-02

## Model

Take the smallest nontrivial multi-special-edge special RAAG:
\[
G=\langle v_1,v_2,w\mid [v_1,v_2]=1,;
wv_iw^{-1}=v_i^{1+q}\ (i=1,2)\rangle.
\]
Thus \(v_1,v_2\) are ordinary origins, \(w\) is the common special/sinkhole vertex, and the ordinary vertices commute.

## Intrinsic extension defect

As in the rank-2 case,
\[
G'=\overline{\langle v_1^q,v_2^q\rangle}\subseteq D_q.
\]
Hence \(W_n\) is abelian for \(n\le q\), and at the first surviving layer
\[
\operatorname{im}\kappa_q
=
\mathbf F_p\overline{v_1^q}
\oplus
\mathbf F_p\overline{v_2^q}.
\]

The q-power map satisfies
\[
P_q(\bar v_i)=\overline{v_i^q},
\qquad
P_q(\bar w)=\overline{w^q}.
\]
Projection to the cyclic quotient generated by w shows that \(P_q(\bar w)\) has a nonzero w^q component, whereas the defect plane contains only the \(v_i^q\)-components. Therefore
\[
P_q^{-1}(\operatorname{im}\kappa_q)
=
\operatorname{span}\{\bar v_1,\bar v_2\}.
\]

Thus the intrinsic defect recovers the entire ordinary/origin plane.

Because \(L_1\) has dimension three, its quotient by this plane is one-dimensional:
\[
L_1/\operatorname{span}\{\bar v_1,\bar v_2\}
\cong \mathbf F_p\bar w.
\]
So in this smallest common-sink model the sinkhole direction is recovered canonically as a quotient line, and here the quotient line is literally one-dimensional, removing the ambiguity that remained in the rank-2 literal-complement formulation.

## Orientation

The canonical orientation is
\[
\theta(v_1)=\theta(v_2)=1,
\qquad
\theta(w)=1+q.
\]
Therefore, once the intrinsic origin plane and q are recovered, the orientation is recovered at the declared family level.

This is still a classification-relative bridge, not a new theorem about arbitrary special RAAGs.

## Critical boundary

The test exposes the real obstruction for the next generalization.

If \([v_1,v_2]\neq1\), then \(G'\) contains a degree-2 ordinary commutator that survives far below q, so \(W_q\) is not abelian. The simple extension commutator pairing \(\kappa_q:W_q\times W_q\to A_q\) is then unavailable as a direct pairing on \(W_q\).

Thus the successful common-sink mechanism depends on an abelianized/commuting origin sector. For a general RAAG, the q-defect must be extracted after quotienting or separating the ordinary degree-2 commutator sector. That is exactly the unresolved general carrier problem.
