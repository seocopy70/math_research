## 1. Why one R9 is not enough

There are two presentation levels that must remain distinct:

- q = infinity baseline: initial quadratic relation only;
- q = 9: the same quadratic relation together with the first q-dependent degree-9 source.

The degree-9 quotient therefore should not be described by a single unexplained symbol R9.

Use:

- I_infty for the restricted ideal generated by the baseline relation;
- I_9 for the restricted ideal generated by the baseline relation together with the q=9 degree-9 initial source, once that source is admitted at the quotient level.

Their degree-9 pieces are I_infty,9 and I_9,9.

This separation is important because S9 = Delta_9(9) = X1^9 is currently only an associative Magnus-word identity.

## 2. Ambient algebra

Let L^res be the free restricted Lie algebra over F3 on X1,...,X4, with the project convention that homogeneous degree is preserved by brackets and the p-map multiplies degree by 3.

Thus:

- [L_i^res, L_j^res] lies in degree i+j;
- (L_i^res)^[3] lies in degree 3i.

The degree-9 ambient is L_9^res, whose project certificate gives dimension 29144.

## 3. Initial baseline relation

Let

R2 = [X1,X2] + [X3,X4].

The ordinary Lie relation layers already audited in the repository are generated recursively:

(R)_3 = [L1,R2],
(R)_4 = [L1,(R)_3],
(R)_5 = [L1,(R)_4],

and not by the old shortcut [L2,R2].

These ordinary layers are retained as the Lie-bracket part of the relation closure.

## 4. Restricted ideal generated by the baseline relation

For any subset S of a restricted Lie algebra, define <S>_res to be the smallest graded subspace containing S and closed under:

1. scalar multiplication;
2. brackets [a,x] with arbitrary ambient a;
3. restricted p-powers x -> x^[3].

Equivalently, it is the intersection of all restricted ideals containing S.

This is the canonical abstract object to use. A restricted ideal must be an ordinary ideal and be closed under the p-map. Standard restricted-Lie references use this definition. See the literature note below.

Define

I_infty := <R2>_res.

Then the baseline degree-9 relation space is

I_infty,9 := I_infty ∩ L_9^res.

No numerical dimension is asserted here.

## 5. Degree recursion useful for computation

Because the algebra is graded, the smallest restricted ideal can be constructed degree-by-degree.

Let I_n be its homogeneous degree-n piece.

Start with

I_2 = span{R2}.

Then I_n is generated by all homogeneous bracket contributions

[L_i^res, I_{n-i}]

and, when 3 divides n, all p-power contributions

(I_{n/3})^[3],

with linear span taken in degree n.

For the baseline through degree 9 this gives the candidate recursion:

I_2 = <R2>,

I_3 = [L1,I_2],

I_4 = [L1,I_3] + [L2,I_2],

I_5 = [L1,I_4] + [L2,I_3] + [L3,I_2],

I_6 = [L1,I_5] + [L2,I_4] + [L3,I_3] + [L4,I_2] + I_2^[3],

and similarly for higher degrees, with

I_9 = sum_{i=1}^8 [L_i^res,I_{9-i}] + (I_3)^[3].

Important: these formulas are a definition of a closure procedure, not yet a claim that all displayed summands are independent or that some are not redundant.

Also, the symbols L_i^res must be interpreted correctly: at degrees divisible by 3, the restricted ambient has p-power layers in addition to the ordinary Lie component.

## 6. Why the old ordinary recursion is not enough

The repository's audited relation recursion through degree 5 is an ordinary-Lie calculation. It establishes the correct bracket-generated layers, but it does not by itself establish the full restricted ideal.

For example, in characteristic 3,

I_2^[3] lies in degree 6.

Its brackets can therefore contribute to degree 9.

Also,

I_3^[3] lies directly in degree 9.

Hence degree 9 cannot be defined merely as an ordinary Lie layer followed by an unexplained p-power term.

The exact redundancy relations among these contributions must be checked.

## 7. q=9 extension

The q=9 source is fixed at the associative Magnus level as

S9 := Delta_9(9) = X1^9.

It is NOT yet identified with X1^[9] inside L_9^res.

Only after an independent quotient-level admissibility check may one define a degree-9 restricted source element s9^res corresponding to S9.

If such an element is admitted, define the q=9 relation ideal conditionally by

I_9 := < R2, s9^res >_res.

Then

I_9,9 = I_9 ∩ L_9^res.

Until the identification of S9 inside the restricted ambient is proved, this remains a conditional definition and is not an implemented quotient.

## 8. Important distinction: ideal closure vs source difference

Do not silently replace

I_9,9

by

I_infty,9 + span{S9}.

The restricted ideal generated by S9 may interact with lower-degree relation closure through brackets and p-powers. At degree 9, the direct contribution of S9 is degree 9, but its interaction with other generators must be considered according to the graded ideal definition.

Likewise, do not assume that the q=9 and q=infinity relation ideals have the same degree-9 part.

## 10. Literature support

A restricted ideal is an ordinary ideal closed under the p-map. A source defining this explicitly is Benayadi et al., where an ideal I of a restricted Lie algebra is called a p-ideal when x^[p] belongs to I for every x in I. The same source describes the smallest p-ideal containing an ordinary ideal via p-power closure. citeturn0search21

The grading behavior needed here is also standard in Jennings/restricted Lie constructions: the p-th power of a homogeneous element of degree d is homogeneous of degree pd. citeturn0search1

These sources support the definition-level distinction only; they do not prove the project's specific R9 dimensions or H-stability.
