# Phase 2-3 — End_H(W) optimized computation

Date: 2026-09-15

## 1. Purpose

Determine the exact endomorphism algebra

\[
\operatorname{End}_H(W)=\{X\in M_{45}(\mathbb F_3): XA_i=A_iX\ \forall i\},
\]

where \(H=Sp_4(\mathbb F_3)\), \(W\) is the 45-dimensional orbit module generated by the target \(T\), and \(A_1,\dots,A_5\) are the five explicit generator action matrices from Phase 2-1.

The Phase 2-1 conventions and construction are reused unchanged.

## 2. Existing data reused

From Phase 2-1:

- \(H=Sp_4(\mathbb F_3)\).
- Five symplectic transvection generators corresponding to
  \[
  e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4,e_1+e_3.
  \]
- These generators have group closure of order
  \[
  |H|=51840.
  \]
- \(\dim Q_4=55\).
- \(\dim W=45\).
- \(\operatorname{rank}[R_4\mid W]=50\).
- \(\dim W^H=0\).
- Five action matrices
  \[
  A_1,\ldots,A_5\in M_{45}(\mathbb F_3)
  \]
  have already been constructed exactly from the quotient coordinates.

## 3. Why the original direct system was too large

A general \(X\in M_{45}(\mathbb F_3)\) has

\[
45^2=2025
\]

unknown scalar entries. The five equations

\[
XA_i=A_iX
\]

produce up to

\[
5\cdot45^2=10125
\]

scalar equations.

Thus the naive system has shape

\[
10125\times2025.
\]

A dense pure-Python Gaussian elimination on that full system previously timed out.

## 4. Centralizer reduction

Instead of solving for all 2025 entries, choose

\[
B=A_2+A_3+A_4+A_5.
\]

Over \(\mathbb F_3\), the Krylov matrix

\[
[I,B,B^2,\ldots,B^{44}]
\]

viewed as 2025-dimensional column vectors has rank

\[
\boxed{45}.
\]

Therefore \(B\) is cyclic (non-derogatory), and its centralizer in \(M_{45}(\mathbb F_3)\) is exactly

\[
C(B)=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb F_3}\{I,B,\ldots,B^{44}\},
\]

of dimension 45.

Every \(H\)-endomorphism commutes with \(B\), so it is necessarily of the form

\[
X=\sum_{k=0}^{44}c_kB^k.
\]

The remaining conditions \([X,A_i]=0\) therefore reduce the calculation to a system with only 45 unknowns and 10125 scalar equations:

\[
E\in M_{10125\times45}(\mathbb F_3).
\]

## 5. Exact computed result

The reduced matrix has

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}(E)=43}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{\dim_{\mathbb F_3}\operatorname{End}_H(W)=45-43=2}.
\]

This is the central Phase 2-3 result.

## 6. Explicit non-scalar endomorphism

A second basis element \(N\) of \(\operatorname{End}_H(W)\), independent of \(I\), was explicitly constructed from the nullspace.

It satisfies

\[
NA_i=A_iN\qquad(i=1,\ldots,5),
\]

and direct verification gives

\[
\boxed{N\ne0},\qquad
\boxed{N^2=0}.
\]

Its rank is

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}N=10},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{\dim\ker N=35},\qquad
\boxed{\dim\operatorname{im}N=10}.
\]

Because \(N\) commutes with every generator, both \(\ker N\) and \(\operatorname{im}N\) are \(H\)-submodules. Since \(N^2=0\),

\[
\operatorname{im}N\subseteq\ker N,
\]

giving the verified submodule chain

\[
0\subsetneq\operatorname{im}N\subsetneq\ker N\subsetneq W
\]

with dimensions

\[
0<10<35<45.
\]

## 7. Algebra structure

Since the endomorphism algebra has dimension 2 and contains \(I\) and a nonzero \(N\) with \(N^2=0\),

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{End}_H(W)\cong \mathbb F_3[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)}.
\]

In particular, it is not a division algebra because it contains the nonzero nilpotent \(N\).

Therefore \(W\) cannot be a simple/irreducible \(H\)-module: Schur's lemma would force the endomorphism ring of a simple finite-dimensional module over \(\mathbb F_3\) to be a division ring.

Moreover, \(\mathbb F_3[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)\) has no nontrivial idempotents. Hence \(W\) has no nontrivial decomposition into a direct sum of two nonzero \(H\)-submodules. Thus the verified structure is:

\[
\boxed{W\text{ is reducible but indecomposable.}}
\]

This is stronger and more precise than merely saying that the earlier 11 cyclic probes did not prove irreducibility.

## 8. Important methodological point

The calculation did **not** assume irreducibility and did not use the 11 coordinate-direction probes as a proof of irreducibility.

The reduction is exact because:

1. every \(H\)-endomorphism commutes with each \(A_i\), hence with \(B=A_2+A_3+A_4+A_5\);
2. \(B\) was directly verified to be cyclic;
3. therefore every matrix commuting with \(B\) is a unique polynomial in \(B\) of degree at most 44;
4. the remaining commutator equations were solved exactly over \(\mathbb F_3\).

Thus the nullity 2 result is an exact finite-field linear-algebra computation, not a probabilistic inference.

## 9. New mathematical consequence

The original target \(T\) did not define a fixed vector under \(Sp_4(\mathbb F_3)\), and in fact

\[
W^H=0.
\]

Phase 2-3 now shows that the 45-dimensional module generated by \(T\) is not simple. Instead it has a nontrivial canonical-looking H-equivariant nilpotent operator \(N\), producing the intrinsic H-submodules

\[
\operatorname{im}N\quad(10\text{ dim}),
\qquad
\ker N\quad(35\text{ dim}).
\]

This shifts the research question. The next problem is no longer merely whether \(T\) is an invariant vector. We should determine the module structures of

\[
\operatorname{im}N,
\qquad
\ker N/\operatorname{im}N,
\qquad
W/\ker N,
\]

and their composition factors.

In particular, the dimensions

\[
10,\quad25,\quad10
\]

are now the first natural candidates for the composition-layer analysis, but **they are not yet identified as irreducible composition factors**. That must be computed, not assumed.
